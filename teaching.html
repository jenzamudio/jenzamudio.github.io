TEACHING & MENTORING

I teach engineering by making students experience it.

I design learning experiences that connect technical concepts to authentic problems, human behavior, and the systems in which engineering decisions take place. My goal is to help students become engineers who can navigate complexity with analytical rigor, empathy, and systems thinking.

HOW I TEACH

Engineering education should be experienced, not simply studied.

My teaching is grounded in experiential and collaborative learning. I want students to encounter the complexity of engineering problems firsthand—to experiment, make decisions, encounter tradeoffs, learn from failure, and reflect on how their decisions affect people and systems.

01

EXPERIENTIAL

Students learn systems concepts by interacting with them. I use simulation, applied problem solving, and hands-on activities to make abstract engineering concepts tangible.

02

COLLABORATIVE

I structure opportunities for students to learn with and from one another through peer discussion, think-pair-share, and structured small-group problem solving.

03

HUMAN-CENTERED

Engineering decisions affect people. I encourage students to consider stakeholder needs, human behavior, communication, and the broader context in which systems operate.

04

REFLECTIVE

I treat teaching as an evidence-based practice, continually refining instruction through reflection, student feedback, and engagement with engineering education research.

PEDAGOGICAL TOOLKIT

Turning systems thinking into something students can do.

I use active learning strategies that allow students to experiment, collaborate, iterate, and connect technical decisions to human and organizational consequences.

Simulation-Based Learning

Immersive simulations that allow students to experience workload, communication, coordination, environmental constraints, and system-level consequences firsthand.

Gamification

Games and structured challenges that create opportunities to experiment, make decisions, encounter tradeoffs, and learn through iteration.

LEGO-Based Systems Activities

Hands-on activities in which students redesign production systems while considering efficiency, communication, stakeholder needs, and worker performance.

Think-Pair-Share

Structured opportunities for students to develop and test their thinking with a peer before sharing ideas with the larger class.

Structured Small-Group Problem Solving

Collaborative activities that expose students to multiple approaches while creating pathways for participation from students with different backgrounds and levels of experience.

Scaffolded Research Experiences

Gradually building students' independence through guided practice in research methods, qualitative analysis, and R programming.

LEARNING GOALS

What I want students to leave with.

01

Systems Thinking

Understand how technical, organizational, and human factors interact within complex systems.

02

Analytical Rigor

Approach ambiguous problems systematically while evaluating evidence, assumptions, and tradeoffs.

03

Human-Centered Design

Ask who a system serves, who it affects, and how engineering decisions shape human experience.

04

Communication Across Disciplines

Communicate effectively with people who bring different expertise, experiences, and perspectives.

05

Comfort With Ambiguity

Develop the confidence to experiment, encounter uncertainty, learn from failure, and iterate.

06

Empathy & Responsibility

Recognize the human consequences of engineering decisions and design systems that create value for the people they affect.
