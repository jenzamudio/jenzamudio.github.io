TEACHING PHILOSOPHY

Learning should connect people, ideas, and practice.

My approach to teaching is grounded in the belief that students learn best when they can connect what they are learning to real problems and experiences. I aim to create learning environments where students feel comfortable asking questions, exploring unfamiliar ideas, and applying what they know to problems that matter.

My research in human-centered systems also shapes how I think about teaching. Rather than treating students as passive recipients of information, I see learning as an interactive process that depends on engagement, feedback, collaboration, and a sense of psychological safety.

I am particularly interested in helping students bridge technical and behavioral perspectives—an approach that reflects my own interdisciplinary work across industrial and operations engineering, human factors, psychology, healthcare, and measurement.
