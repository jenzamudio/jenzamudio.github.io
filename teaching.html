TEACHING & MENTORING

Designing learning environments where students can experience systems thinking.

As I develop my academic career, I hope to build courses that bring together human factors, systems engineering, behavioral science, and real-world applications. I want students to leave not only with strong technical skills, but with the ability to understand the human context of engineering problems and design systems that improve people's lives. \

HOW I TEACH

Engineering education should be experienced, not simply studied.

My teaching is grounded in experiential and collaborative learning. I want students to encounter the complexity of engineering problems firsthand—to experiment, make decisions, encounter tradeoffs, learn from failure, and reflect on how their decisions affect people and systems.

EXPERIENTIAL

Students learn systems concepts by interacting with them. I use simulation, applied problem solving, and hands-on activities to make abstract engineering concepts tangible.

COLLABORATIVE

I structure opportunities for students to learn with and from one another through peer discussion, think-pair-share, and structured small-group problem solving.

HUMAN-CENTERED

Engineering decisions affect people. I encourage students to consider stakeholder needs, human behavior, communication, and the broader context in which systems operate.

REFLECTIVE

I treat teaching as an evidence-based practice, continually refining instruction through reflection, student feedback, and engagement with engineering education research.
