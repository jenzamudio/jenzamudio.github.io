CURRENT RESEARCH

I study the human side of complex systems.

Healthcare and workplaces are not simply technical systems. They are environments where clinicians and patients communicate, make decisions, build relationships, experience stress, and depend on one another. The connections built are important for both parties.

My research examines how the design of work and healthcare systems shapes clinician well-being, patient experiences, and the quality of interactions between them. I focus particularly on how organizational and interactional conditions influence the connections that develop between doctors and patients, centering the patient’s perspective. Ultimately, I aim to develop evidence-based approaches for designing systems that foster meaningful relationships, well-being, and safety through a justice-oriented lens.

My work brings together industrial and operations engineering, human factors, organizational psychology, and measurement science to understand these experiences and translate them into opportunities for system design.

A central question across my research is:

How can we design systems that make meaningful human connection more possible? What barriers exist within the system itself to achieve this?

RESEARCH AREAS

Three connected areas guide my current work.

02

Work, Wellbeing & Connection

Examining how relationships and everyday interactions shape wellbeing at work, with an emphasis on designing work environments where people can connect, thrive, and perform.

Wellbeing · Work design · Relationships · Organizational psychology
01

Patient–Provider Connection

Understanding how patients experience connection with their healthcare providers and what behaviors, processes, and relational experiences create or disrupt that connection.

Patient experience · Healthcare relationships · Communication · Connection
03

Measurement Science & Patient-Centered Outcomes

Developing rigorous ways to measure complex human experiences and using those measures to inform the design and evaluation of healthcare and work systems.

Connection is frequently discussed as an important part of healthcare, but it is difficult to evaluate systematically. This work focuses on developing rigorous measures of the patient–provider relational experience.

I draw on qualitative findings and existing theories of relational connection to identify dimensions of connection and translate them into measurable constructs and survey items.

The long-term goal is a measure that can be used not only to describe patient experiences, but also to evaluate whether interventions actually improve connection.

Measurement · Human factors · Scale development · Systems design
