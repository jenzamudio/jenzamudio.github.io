CURRENT RESEARCH
I study the human side of interactions
within complex systems.

Healthcare and workplaces are more than technical systems. They are human environments where people communicate, make decisions, build relationships, and depend on one another. Bringing together industrial and operations engineering, human factors, organizational psychology, and measurement science, advised by Dr. X. Jessie Yang in the Interaction & Collaboration Research Lab study these experiences and translate what we learn into opportunities for better system design. At the center of my work is a simple question:

How can we design systems that make meaningful connection more possible—and remove the barriers that stand in its way?

This work examined the internist perspective on X.

This work reinforced the idea that work system design influences internist well-being by taking away time from direct patient care and forms the foundation for my broader research on exploring what connection means to patients. RESEARCH AREA

Patient-Clinician Connection

I am interested in understanding how patients conceptualize what it means to feel connected or disconnected from their clinicians.

PROJECT

Exploring Patient Perspectives of Connection and Disconnection

This project explore 200+ open-ended survey responses from hospitalized patients describing what it means to them to feel connected or disconnected from their healthcare providers.

03

RESEARCH AREA

Patient-Centered Outcomes

I develop rigorous approaches for measuring complex human experiences and translating those measures into tools that can inform the design and evaluation of healthcare and organizational systems.

PROJECT

Measuring Patient–Doctor Connection

Connection is frequently discussed as an important part of healthcare, but it is difficult to evaluate systematically. This work focuses on developing rigorous measures of the patient–doctor relational experience.

I draw on qualitative findings and existing theories of relational connection to identify dimensions of connection and translate them into measurable constructs and survey items.

FUTURE RESEARCH

I am interested in research that crosses disciplinary boundaries.

I am particularly interested in collaborations that bring together engineering, psychology, organizational science, and community perspectives to address complex human and organizational challenges.

Building on my expertise in human factors engineering, psychology, and measurement science, I aim to develop a research program that examines how organizational and clinical environments shape human experiences, and works collaboratively with communities to design more equitable solutions.
