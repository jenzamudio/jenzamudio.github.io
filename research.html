CURRENT RESEARCH
I study the human side of complex systems.

Healthcare and workplaces are more than technical systems. They are human environments where people communicate, make decisions, build relationships, and depend on one another. How these systems are designed shapes those experiences.

My research examines how the design of work and healthcare systems influences clinician well-being, patient experiences, and the interactions between them. I focus on how system and organizational conditions shape meaningful connection between doctors and patients, centering the patient perspective.

Bringing together industrial and operations engineering, human factors, organizational psychology, and measurement science, I study these experiences and translate what we learn into opportunities for better system design.

At the center of my work is a simple question:

How can we design systems that make meaningful connection more possible—and remove the barriers that stand in its way?

RESEARCH AREAS

Three connected areas guide my current work.

02

Work, Wellbeing & Connection

Examining how relationships and everyday interactions shape wellbeing at work, with an emphasis on designing work environments where people can connect, thrive, and perform.

Wellbeing · Work design · Relationships · Organizational psychology
01

Patient–Provider Connection

Understanding how patients experience connection with their healthcare providers and what behaviors, processes, and relational experiences create or disrupt that connection.

Patient experience · Healthcare relationships · Communication · Connection
03

Measurement Science & Patient-Centered Outcomes

Developing rigorous ways to measure complex human experiences and using those measures to inform the design and evaluation of healthcare and work systems.

Connection is frequently discussed as an important part of healthcare, but it is difficult to evaluate systematically. This work focuses on developing rigorous measures of the patient–provider relational experience.

I draw on qualitative findings and existing theories of relational connection to identify dimensions of connection and translate them into measurable constructs and survey items.

The long-term goal is a measure that can be used not only to describe patient experiences, but also to evaluate whether interventions actually improve connection.

Measurement · Human factors · Scale development · Systems design

FUTURE RESEARCH

I am interested in research that crosses disciplinary boundaries.

I am particularly interested in collaborations that bring together engineering, psychology, organizational science, and community perspectives to address complex human and organizational challenges.

Building on my expertise in human factors engineering, psychology, and measurement science, I aim to develop a research program that examines how organizational and clinical environments shape human experiences, and works collaboratively with communities to design more equitable solutions.
