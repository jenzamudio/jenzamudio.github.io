Research

Human connection, measurement, and human-centered systems.

My research examines how relationships and system design shape wellbeing, performance, and experiences of care.

01

Patient-Provider Connection

How do patients and providers experience connection—and how can healthcare systems support it?

My dissertation research investigates the processes through which patients and clinicians experience connection and disconnection during healthcare interactions. This work combines qualitative inquiry, measurement development, and computational approaches.

Qualitative research

Identifying the interactional processes and relational experiences patients describe as meaningful.

Measurement

Developing tools to rigorously assess patient-provider connection.

Design

Translating findings into intervention and system-design opportunities.

02

Measurement Science

How can complex human experiences be measured rigorously?

I am interested in the translation of rich human experiences into reliable and valid quantitative measures, particularly when constructs are relational, contextual, or difficult to observe directly.

Scale developmentContent validityPsychometricsQualitative-to-quantitative methodsComputational methods
03

Human-Centered Systems

How can we redesign systems to better support the people within them?

My broader research agenda extends these ideas into healthcare and work systems, with an emphasis on wellbeing, connection, human factors, and designing environments that support effective human performance.
