RESEARCH

Designing systems for connection, wellbeing, and meaningful work.

My research examines how human connection shapes experiences in healthcare and work, and how we can design systems that support better relationships, wellbeing, and performance.

RESEARCH PROGRAM

I study the human side of complex systems.

Healthcare and workplaces are not simply technical systems. They are environments where people communicate, make decisions, build relationships, experience stress, and depend on one another.

My work brings together industrial and operations engineering, human factors, organizational psychology, healthcare research, and measurement science to understand these experiences and translate them into opportunities for system design.

A central question across my research is:

How can we design systems that make meaningful human connection more possible?

CURRENT & RECENT PROJECTS

From understanding connection to designing for it.

My projects move from understanding people's experiences, to measuring those experiences, to using what we learn to improve the systems in which people work and receive care.

DISSERTATION

Understanding, Measuring, and Quantifying Patient–Provider Connection

My dissertation examines the ways patients describe their connection with healthcare providers and seeks to understand the processes and experiences that make that connection possible.

The work combines qualitative research, measurement development, and computational approaches. Across multiple phases, I examine how connection is experienced, how it can be measured, and how patterns of connection and disconnection can be quantified.

Ultimately, this work aims to move connection from an abstract idea to something that can be understood, measured, and intentionally supported within healthcare systems.

SETTING Healthcare
METHODS Qualitative · Measurement · Computational
FOCUS Patient experience · Patient safety · Relational connection

MEASUREMENT SCIENCE

Developing Measures of Patient–Provider Connection

Connection is frequently discussed as an important part of healthcare, but it is difficult to evaluate systematically. This work focuses on developing rigorous measures of the patient–provider relational experience.

I draw on qualitative findings and existing theories of relational connection to identify dimensions of connection and translate them into measurable constructs and survey items.

The long-term goal is a measure that can be used not only to describe patient experiences, but also to evaluate whether interventions actually improve connection.

SETTING Healthcare
METHODS Scale development · Psychometrics · Qualitative research
FOCUS Measurement · Patient experience

COMPUTATIONAL APPROACHES

Identifying Patterns of Connection and Disconnection

Human connection is multidimensional. Patients may describe feeling understood, cared for, respected, heard, or supported, while also describing specific behaviors that contribute to those experiences.

This work uses computational and quantitative approaches to examine how these experiences co-occur and whether different patterns of relational experiences emerge across healthcare settings.

By quantifying these patterns, the project explores how complex qualitative experiences can inform more systematic approaches to understanding connection.

SETTING Healthcare
METHODS Computational analysis · Qualitative coding · Quantitative analysis
FOCUS Relational patterns · Connection · Patient experience

WORK & WELLBEING

Designing Work Systems That Support Connection

A natural extension of my healthcare research is examining how connection operates within workplaces more broadly.

This emerging line of work considers how everyday work design, interpersonal interactions, and organizational systems influence wellbeing and people's ability to experience meaningful connection at work.

I am particularly interested in using measures of connection to evaluate interventions and redesign work systems in ways that support both human wellbeing and organizational performance.

SETTING Workplaces
METHODS Human-centered design · Organizational research · Measurement
FOCUS Wellbeing · Work design · Connection
